# How to use this deck
Read About this page and Target keywords first. Then write in the shaded Your copy column, one field at a time.

Budgets are the most you can use, in characters with spaces unless marked as words. They are ceilings, not targets: a field that does its job in fewer characters is done. The one exception is the pull quote: a strong quote over budget is a note for Banyan, not a cut. Claude in Word can count every field for you, or select the cell and use Review > Word Count > Characters (with spaces). Enter the number in Count.

Direction says what each field is for and what the reader needs from it. The words are yours. A cell that is already filled in is set by the IA; leave it as it is.

Figures and quotes come from Material you may use, near the end of this deck. You may also use a figure or quote published on aopa.org; note its link in Questions/Notes. Leave accident statistics out unless Material lists them: several on aopa.org are under review with AOPA.

You may use other blocks from the build team's Gutenberg block library where they help the page. Flag it in Questions/Notes so the page builder knows.

Questions and notes go in the Questions/Notes table at the end, not in the copy. Track changes is on.

Keep open alongside: the wireframe, a visual for the layout of this page; the AOPA Web Writing Reference; and the appendix brief if you want more depth.

Where the appendix and this deck disagree, this deck wins. It already reflects the reviewer comments in the appendix, and its search terms have been checked against live results pages.

The AOPA Self-Editor skill suggests; you decide. Point it at your copy fields only, then accept or reject each tracked change and comment it leaves.

Spell out every acronym the first time it appears on the page, with the abbreviation in parentheses: Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), visual flight rules (VFR). After that the abbreviation alone is fine. If a field can't hold the full name, put it in the meta description. Name the institute in full the first time, the AOPA Air Safety Institute, and Air Safety Institute after that.

Where the page talks about general aviation, write it in full in the SEO title, meta description, headline, the first sentence of the body copy, image alt text and card descriptions, and again as general aviation (GA) at the start of any section that uses GA. After that, GA works on its own or before a noun. This is a guideline, not a rule: use your judgment where the full words would spoil a headline or a quote is at its budget.

## Intro
Do the Right Thing course: FAA Wings eligible with an AOPA login, free or paid (credit is submitted to the FAA automatically when the course is added to the pilot's Air Safety Institute transcript; the email on the AOPA web account must match the FAA Wings account), Accident Forgiveness eligible, tablet compatible. Source: aopa.org/training-and-safety/online-learning/online-courses/do-the-right-thing; login: confirmed by the AOPA Air Safety Institute in comments on the brief, September 2026.

Safety Spotlights are free to read; the certificate for the transcript needs a free AOPA account; no Safety Spotlight earns Wings credit. Source: confirmed by the AOPA Air Safety Institute in comments on the brief, September 2026.

Accident Forgiveness: “Available on select insurance policies underwritten by” AIG Aerospace, United States Aircraft Insurance Group (USAIG), Old Republic Aerospace, Starr Aviation, Global Aerospace and U.S. Specialty Insurance Company (USSIC). A pilot on one of those policies qualifies by completing qualifying Air Safety Institute safety education every six months. Source: aopa.org/training-and-safety/air-safety-institute/air-safety-institute-accident-forgiveness
## Cards

Source: aopa.org/training-and-safety/online-learning/safety-spotlights/do-the-right-thing. The course covers decision habits, the accident chain, personal minimums, external pressure and contingency planning (course source under Intro). Personal minimums: VFR and IFR Personal Minimums Contracts. Source: aopa.org/training-and-safety/online-learning/safety-spotlights/do-the-right-thing/personal-minimums

Source: aopa.org/training-and-safety/air-safety-institute/mental-health-resource-center
